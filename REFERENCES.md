# Design references — patterns, not pixels
Drop this file in the repo root. Rule zero: if a visitor could name the site we borrowed from, we borrowed too much. Extract at most 3 concrete tokens/patterns per reference into the human-design system; never screenshot-to-code.

## 1. McMaster-Carr (mcmaster.com) → the grocery list + plan views
The most beloved "ugly" site on the internet, because it isn't ugly — it's pure function at maximum speed.
**Steal:** information density done calmly (tight rows, hairline rules, zero decoration between the user and the data); instant page loads as a design feature; every element earns its pixels.
**Don't take:** the actual visual style — we're warmer. Take the philosophy: on the list view, nothing exists that doesn't help you shop.

## 2. Cash App (cash.app) → how money numbers feel
A money brand with actual personality — custom mono numerals, huge confident figures, playful without being childish.
**Steal:** numbers as the hero graphic (big tabular-mono totals doing the job illustrations do elsewhere); brand courage — one weird-but-owned choice per surface; short human copy next to big numbers.
**Don't take:** their green, their gradients-adjacent moments, or the street-culture tone — our voice is "person who counted," not "brand that vibes."

## 3. Mercury (mercury.com) → the landing page
The reference for looking expensive while completely flat: editorial serif/grotesque pairing, enormous whitespace, restraint as luxury.
**Steal:** confidence through subtraction (one idea per section, no icon grids); real product UI shown honestly instead of abstract illustration; muted palette + one accent used maybe three times per page.
**Don't take:** the banking gravitas — we're $52 at Aldi, not treasury management. Keep their restraint, swap in warmth.

## 4. The Receiptify trend (Spotify-stats-as-receipt) → the ledger share card
Proof our core artifact is inherently viral: millions of people voluntarily posted their music stats *because* they were formatted as a thermal receipt.
**Steal:** the exact shareable unit — narrow receipt card, dotted leaders, uppercase micro-labels, a barcode-ish footer, dated and personal; designed to be screenshotted at phone-story ratio.
**Don't take:** novelty-only execution. Ours carries real money data, so legibility beats gimmick — every number on the card must be true.

## 5. Teenage Engineering (teenage.engineering) → product/spec surfaces (pricing page, staple database view)
Industrial minimalism where the spec sheet IS the aesthetic: mono labels, hairlines, lowercase, ruthless grid.
**Steal:** spec-sheet presentation for the pricing tiers and the staple pool ("what's in the box" energy); lowercase micro-labels; the confidence to leave large areas empty.
**Don't take:** the coldness. One playful receipt-tilt or stamp keeps us human where TE stays sterile.

## 6. Linear (linear.app) → craft discipline, not look
Wrong palette for us (dark, glassy) but the industry standard for finish.
**Steal:** the discipline — obsessive spacing consistency within a section, copy with zero filler words, focus states and hover details that feel considered, changelog-style honesty in text.
**Don't take:** literally any visual token. Dark glass + glow on WiseDinner would be the exact AI-slop tell we banned.

## How to apply (for Claude Code sessions)
1. Before any UI job, skim this file after the human-design skill.
2. Per surface, apply its mapped reference's "steal" list only. Never mix more than two references on one page.
3. The one-glance test before commit: would a stranger say "this looks like [site]"? If yes, pull back until they'd only say "this looks intentional."
